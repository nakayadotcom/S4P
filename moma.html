Sound Is a Creative Material for Children's Play

Learn more about the worlds kids can make from and through sound

Louisa Penfold • Dec 2, 2024

Ever since Frederick Froebel created the first kindergarten in 1837 Germany, playing with materials has been a huge part of early-years learning. As children make, play, and experiment with materials such as blocks, paint, clay, sand, cardboard, paper, and light, these interactions open up new and divergent learning processes.

Sound as Expression

Like young children, artists also play with materials and express themselves in multisensory ways. Sound, in particular, is a creative material that has captured the imagination of many artists since the beginning of the 20th century.

Susan Philipsz, a Scottish sound artist, once said: "Sound is materially invisible but very visceral and emotive. It can define a space at the same time as it triggers a memory."

Deep and Active Listening

Something special about sound art is that it encourages us to attend to sounds in new ways, which can help children develop aural awareness and the vocabulary to describe these differences. Creating art with sound also encourages children to engage in deep and active listening.

The Rainforest Legacy

One of the inspirations for MoMA's new Art Lab: Sound, is David Tudor's Rainforest IV. In 1973, Tudor created a large-scale immersive sound installation that transformed everyday objects such as cans, pots, plastic pipes, and speakers into sculptural forms.

Activity: Sound Mapping

Ask children to write down all the sounds they can hear and the times they hear them. Then ask them to draw a map and mark the location of the sounds on it. This encourages children to attend to the hidden layers of their environment.
